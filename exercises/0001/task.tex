\section{The Chevalley-Warning Theorem and the Cauchy-Davenport Lemma}
We start with a \emph{simplified} version of the Cauchy-Davenport Lemma:
\begin{theorem}[Cauchy-Davenport Lemma]\label{cauchy_davenport_lemma}
  Let $p$ be a prime and $A$, $B$ be two nonempty subsets of $\Zb_p$, then
  $|A+B|\geq \min(p,|A|+|B|-1)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  We use induction in $|B|$ here, where $|A|\geq|B|$ is assumed.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item If $|B|=1$, we can write $B=\{b\}$, and $A+B=A+b$.
    \item Otherwise, assume $A\cap B$ is non-empty and proper to $B$, and apply
          the hypothesis to $A'=A\cup B$ and $B'=A\cap B$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\section{Lucas's Theorem}
Let $m$ and $n$ be non-negative integers and $p$ be a prime. Then:
$$\binom{m}{n} \equiv \prod_{i=0}^{k} \binom{m_i}{n_i} \pmod{p}.$$
As stated in Theorem~\ref{cauchy_davenport_lemma}, the bound is sharp.
